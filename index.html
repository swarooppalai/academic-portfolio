01 / ABOUT

Research Profile & Biography

I am an experimental physicist specializing in quantum optics and nanophotonics, currently working at the intersection of quantum emitters and nanoscale photonic architectures.

My research focuses on controlling and enhancing the emission properties of nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in nanodiamonds by coupling them with dielectric and plasmonic metasurfaces. I bring deep expertise in AFM nanoxerography for deterministic nanoscale assembly, cryogenic spectroscopy, and custom optical instrumentation.

Prior to my postdoctoral work, my PhD at CNRS-LNCMI pioneered AFM ironing as a technique to improve the intrinsic optical quality of 2D semiconductor heterostructures, contributing to publications in Nano Letters and 2D Materials.

Experimental Instrumentation · Free-Space Optics

The Experimental Art of Beam Walking: Decoupling Spatial and Angular Degrees of Freedom

Dr. Swaroop Kumar Palai · CEMES & LPCNO Toulouse

A fundamental rite of passage in the ultrafast and quantum optics laboratory is the alignment of a laser beam through two spatial apertures or into a single-mode fiber coupler. A freely propagating laser beam possesses four spatial degrees of freedom: horizontal and vertical positions $(x, y)$, alongside their respective angular trajectories $(\theta_x, \theta_y)$. A single steering mirror cannot independently control both coordinate sets without introducing severe clipping.

To overcome this, experimentalists employ the classic two-mirror beam steering configuration. Mirror 1 ($M_1$) primarily dictates the near-field position of the beam at Pinhole 1 ($P_1$), whereas Mirror 2 ($M_2$) controls the far-field angle directing into Pinhole 2 ($P_2$). However, because rotating $M_2$ pivots the beam about its reflective surface, each angular adjustment slightly translates the beam off the center of $P_1$, coupling the two variables.

$$\eta_{\text{total}} = \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta y_1^2}{w_1^2}\right) \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta y_2^2}{w_2^2}\right) \to 1.0$$ Collinear transmission efficiency through dual pinhole apertures ($P_1, P_2$)

Experimental convergence is achieved through an iterative feedback algorithm known as beam walking: one adjusts $M_1$ to center through $P_1$, tilts $M_2$ to optimize $P_2$, walks $M_1$ slightly past the maximum to compensate for cross-talk, and repeats until the beam is collinearly locked to the optical axis.

Lab Insight · Cryogenic Confocal Spectroscopy

During low-temperature photoluminescence experiments at 4 Kelvin, mechanical thermal contraction of the optical cryostat introduces sub-milliradian beam walking drift. Mastering fast, deterministic two-mirror compensation ensures optimal confocal excitation and single-photon collection yields.

2D Quantum Materials · Excitonic Physics

Coulomb Confinement in Flatland: Exciton Binding and Linewidth Recovery via AFM Ironing

Dr. Swaroop Kumar Palai · Nano Letters (2023) & 2D Materials (2024)

In atomically thin transition metal dichalcogenides (monolayer $\text{WSe}_2, \text{MoS}_2$), spatial quantum confinement and the abrupt dielectric mismatch between the monolayer and its environment suppress out-of-plane Coulomb field line screening. As a result, the attractive interaction between photogenerated electrons and holes is enormously amplified, forming Wannier-Mott excitons with binding energies exceeding $E_b \approx 300\text{--}500\text{ meV}$—nearly two orders of magnitude greater than in bulk GaAs.

The exciton's real-space wavefunction exhibits a compact Bohr radius ($a_B \approx 1\text{--}3\text{ nm}$). In van der Waals heterostructures, artificial moiré superlattices formed by twist-angle misalignment introduce localized periodic potentials. However, mechanical dry transfer inevitably traps hydrocarbon residues, interfacial bubbles, and non-uniform micro-strain gradients across the junction.

$$E_b \propto \frac{\mu}{\varepsilon_{\text{eff}}^2}, \quad \text{FWHM} = \Gamma_0(T) + \sigma_{\text{strain}}(\nabla\epsilon)$$ Dielectric screening of binding energy and strain-induced inhomogeneous linewidth broadening

These local strain variations deform the bandgap across space, causing severe inhomogeneous photoluminescence (PL) broadening ($\text{FWHM} > 30\text{ meV}$) and obfuscating delicate moiré exciton mini-bands.

Palai et al., Nano Letters 23 (11), 4749–4755 (2023)

We pioneered Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) Ironing: applying a high-deflection cantilever raster-scan across the heterostructure to mechanically squeegee trapped interfacial blister bubbles out of the junction. This planarizes the van der Waals interface, recovers atomic-scale flatness, and sharpens the low-temperature PL linewidth down to its intrinsic 3 meV limit.

Single Spins in Diamond: Optical Spin Readout via Optically Detected Magnetic Resonance (ODMR)

Dr. Swaroop Kumar Palai · Nanophotonics (Wiley 2026) & CEMES-CNRS

The negatively charged nitrogen-vacancy ($\text{NV}^-$) center in diamond behaves as an isolated atom trapped within the rigid, wide-bandgap ($5.5\text{ eV}$) carbon lattice. Possessing $C_{3v}$ trigonal symmetry, the center's ground state is an orbital singlet and spin triplet ($^3A_2, S=1$). Relativistic spin-spin dipolar coupling induces a zero-field splitting of $D \approx 2.87\text{ GHz}$ separating the $m_s = 0$ state from the degenerate $m_s = \pm 1$ states.

Under non-resonant $532\text{ nm}$ green laser excitation, electrons are pumped into the $^3E$ triplet excited state. The optical decay exhibits a crucial spin-dependent asymmetry: while the $m_s = 0$ state predominantly decays radiatively back to $m_s = 0$ by emitting red photons ($637\text{ nm}$ Zero-Phonon Line and phonon sideband), the $m_s = \pm 1$ state exhibits a high probability of intersystem crossing (ISC) into a long-lived metastable singlet state ($^1A_1 \to ^1E$, lifetime $\sim 250\text{ ns}$).

$$\Delta f_{\text{Zeeman}} = 2 \gamma_e B_z \quad (\gamma_e \approx 28\text{ MHz/mT})$$ Zeeman spectral splitting between $m_s = +1$ and $m_s = -1$ spin sublevels

This non-radiative singlet detour shelves the electron, dramatically dimming photoluminescence and polarising the center into the $m_s = 0$ state with $>80\%$ fidelity. By sweeping a resonant microwave field ($f_{\text{MW}} \approx 2.87\text{ GHz}$), one drives the $m_s = 0 \leftrightarrow \pm 1$ transition, generating an immediate drop in fluorescence—the hallmark of Optically Detected Magnetic Resonance (ODMR). An applied magnetic field $B_z$ lifts the $m_s = \pm 1$ degeneracy, splitting the resonance into twin dips whose frequency separation directly measures local magnetic fields with nanotesla sensitivity.

On-Chip Directional Quantum Emission

Coupling deterministic single NV centers into inverse-designed silicon nanoantennas allows us to redirect their broad emission into narrow forward lobes while preserving pristine spin coherence for room-temperature quantum sensing.

Deterministic Nanoxerography: Electrostatic Electrets for Directed Single-Emitter Placement

Dr. Swaroop Kumar Palai · CEMES-CNRS & LPCNO Toulouse

A central bottleneck in hybrid quantum photonics is the stochastic spatial distribution of quantum emitters when deposited via conventional drop-casting or spin-coating. Integrating single nanodiamonds hosting NV centers into the tight near-field hot-spots of plasmonic cavities or dielectric Mie resonators requires sub-50 nanometer positioning precision with high deterministic yield.

AFM Nanoxerography leverages atomic force microscopy to create localized electrostatic potential traps on dielectric films ($\text{SiO}_2$ or PMMA). A conductive, metal-coated AFM tip acts as a nanoscopic electrode. By applying a bias voltage pulse ($V_{\text{bias}} \approx 10\text{--}40\text{ V}$, duration $5\text{--}100\text{ ms}$), charge carriers are injected into deep dielectric traps, generating stable surface charge electrets of controlled diameter and charge density.

$$\vec{F}_{\text{DEP}} = 2\pi\varepsilon_m r^3 \text{Re}[K(\omega)] \nabla |\vec{E}|^2 + q_{\text{zeta}} \vec{E}$$ Dielectrophoretic and Coulomb forces capturing charged nanodiamonds into the electret trap

When the patterned substrate is immersed into a colloidal liquid suspension containing charged nanodiamonds, long-range Coulomb attraction and strong dielectrophoretic gradient forces pull diffusing particles directly into the center of the electret potential well, immobilizing single nanodiamonds at pre-designed coordinates with nanometric spatial resolution.

Directed Assembly at CEMES & LPCNO

By modulating tip bias voltages and colloidal surface functionalization, our group achieved reproducible single-nanodiamond trapping yields over 85%, eliminating multi-particle clusters and enabling the deterministic creation of hybrid single-photon sources.

Quantum Optics · Single-Photon Characterization

Proving Single Photons: The Hanbury Brown–Twiss Interferometer and g⁽²⁾(τ) Antibunching

Dr. Swaroop Kumar Palai · Single-Photon Emission Benchmarking

How can an experimentalist unequivocally prove that an emitter produces genuine quantum light rather than faint classical attenuation? The definitive experimental test is the second-order intensity correlation function, $g^{(2)}(\tau)$, measured using a Hanbury Brown–Twiss (HBT) interferometer.

In an HBT setup, photoluminescence from a targeted nano-object is directed through a $50:50$ non-polarizing beam splitter onto two Single-Photon Avalanche Diodes (SPADs: Start and Stop channels). A Time-Correlated Single-Photon Counting (TCSPC) module records the histogram of arrival delay times $\tau = t_2 - t_1$.

$$g^{(2)}(\tau) = 1 - \left(1 - g^{(2)}(0)\right) e^{-|\tau|/\tau_1}, \quad g^{(2)}(0) = 1 - \frac{1}{N} \rho^2$$ Second-order correlation dip for $N$ identical quantum emitters with background purity $\rho = S/(S+B)$

For classical electromagnetic fields, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality dictates that $g^{(2)}(0) \ge 1$ (coherent laser light yields $g^{(2)}(\tau) = 1$, whereas thermal light exhibits photon bunching $g^{(2)}(0) = 2$). However, a true two-level quantum emitter cannot radiate two photons at the same instant; following emission, it must be re-excited before emitting again. Consequently, the simultaneous coincidence probability at zero delay drops to zero:

g⁽²⁾(0) < 0.5 &implies; Unambiguous Single-Photon Source (Antibunched)

When multiple emitters ($N \ge 2$) reside within the confocal volume, $g^{(2)}(0)$ scales as $1 - 1/N$, exceeding the quantum threshold. Real-world background luminescence ($S/B$) and detector timing jitter convolve the dip, making nanoscale engineering crucial for maximizing quantum purity.

Purcell Enhancement & Background Suppression

Coupling deterministically placed single nanodiamonds to optical metasurfaces accelerates the spontaneous emission rate via the Purcell effect, shortening excited state lifetimes down to <5 ns and elevating signal-to-noise ratios to yield record room-temperature antibunching dips ($g^{(2)}(0) < 0.12$).
