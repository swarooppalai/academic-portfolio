Coherent Spin Readout: Optically Detected Magnetic Resonance in Diamond

Negatively charged nitrogen-vacancy (NV⁻) centers in nanodiamond provide atomic-scale quantum magnetometers. We demonstrate continuous-wave optical polarization into the ms=0 spin state, microwave ESR sweep at 2.87 GHz, and optical detection of Zeeman sublevel splitting.

Dark mode
Solid-State Spin Qubits · Quantum Magnetometry
Dr. Swaroop Kumar Palai · Nanophotonics (Wiley 2026) & CEMES-CNRS

The negatively charged nitrogen-vacancy ($\text{NV}^-$) center in diamond behaves as an isolated atom trapped within the rigid, wide-bandgap ($5.5\text{ eV}$) carbon lattice. Possessing $C_{3v}$ trigonal symmetry, the center's ground state is an orbital singlet and spin triplet ($^3A_2, S=1$). Relativistic spin-spin dipolar coupling induces a zero-field splitting of $D \approx 2.87\text{ GHz}$ separating the $m_s = 0$ state from the degenerate $m_s = \pm 1$ states.

Under non-resonant $532\text{ nm}$ green laser excitation, electrons are pumped into the $^3E$ triplet excited state. The optical decay exhibits a crucial spin-dependent asymmetry: while the $m_s = 0$ state predominantly decays radiatively back to $m_s = 0$ by emitting red photons ($637\text{ nm}$ Zero-Phonon Line and phonon sideband), the $m_s = \pm 1$ state exhibits a high probability of intersystem crossing (ISC) into a long-lived metastable singlet state ($^1A_1 \to ^1E$, lifetime $\sim 250\text{ ns}$).

$$\Delta f_{\text{Zeeman}} = 2 \gamma_e B_z \quad (\gamma_e \approx 28\text{ MHz/mT})$$ Zeeman spectral splitting between $m_s = +1$ and $m_s = -1$ spin sublevels

This non-radiative singlet detour shelves the electron, dramatically dimming photoluminescence and polarising the center into the $m_s = 0$ state with $>80\%$ fidelity. By sweeping a resonant microwave field ($f_{\text{MW}} \approx 2.87\text{ GHz}$), one drives the $m_s = 0 \leftrightarrow \pm 1$ transition, generating an immediate drop in fluorescence—the hallmark of Optically Detected Magnetic Resonance (ODMR). An applied magnetic field $B_z$ lifts the $m_s = \pm 1$ degeneracy, splitting the resonance into twin dips whose frequency separation directly measures local magnetic fields with nanotesla sensitivity.

On-Chip Directional Quantum Emission

Coupling deterministic single NV centers into inverse-designed silicon nanoantennas allows us to redirect their broad emission into narrow forward lobes while preserving pristine spin coherence for room-temperature quantum sensing.
