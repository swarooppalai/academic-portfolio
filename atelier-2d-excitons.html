ATELIER TOPIC 02 · 2D Materials · Moiré Superlattices

Strain Recovery & Exciton Dehybridization in Twisted TMDC Bilayers

Interfacial hydrocarbon bubbles and inhomogeneous strain quench moiré optical quality in van der Waals heterostructures. We examine contact-mode AFM mechanical ironing to sweep defects, recover sub-nanometer atomic contact, and sharpen momentum-indirect exciton linewidths in MoSe₂/MoS₂.

2D Quantum Materials · Excitonic Physics
Dr. Swaroop Kumar Palai · Nano Letters (2023) & 2D Materials (2024)

In atomically thin transition metal dichalcogenides (monolayer WSe2, MoS2), spatial quantum confinement and the abrupt dielectric mismatch between the monolayer and its environment suppress out-of-plane Coulomb field line screening. As a result, the attractive interaction between photogenerated electrons and holes is enormously amplified, forming Wannier-Mott excitons with binding energies exceeding Eb ≈ 300–500 meV—nearly two orders of magnitude greater than in bulk GaAs.

The exciton's real-space wavefunction exhibits a compact Bohr radius (aB ≈ 1–3 nm). In van der Waals heterostructures, artificial moiré superlattices formed by twist-angle misalignment introduce localized periodic potentials. However, mechanical dry transfer inevitably traps hydrocarbon residues, interfacial bubbles, and non-uniform micro-strain gradients across the junction.

Eb ∝ μ / εeff2,    FWHM = Γ0(T) + σstrain(∇ε)
Dielectric screening of binding energy and strain-induced inhomogeneous linewidth broadening

These local strain variations deform the bandgap across space, causing severe inhomogeneous photoluminescence (PL) broadening (FWHM > 30 meV) and obfuscating delicate moiré exciton mini-bands.

Palai et al., Nano Letters 23 (11), 4749–4755 (2023)

We pioneered Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) Ironing: applying a high-deflection cantilever raster-scan across the heterostructure to mechanically squeegee trapped interfacial blister bubbles out of the junction. This planarizes the van der Waals interface, recovers atomic-scale flatness, and sharpens the low-temperature PL linewidth down to its intrinsic 3 meV limit.
