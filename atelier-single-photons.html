ATELIER TOPIC 05 · Quantum Optics · Photon Statistics

HBT Interferometry: Verifying Single-Photon Antibunching

To prove that an emission source is a true quantum emitter rather than an ensemble, we measure the second-order photon autocorrelation function g⁽²⁾(τ) using a Hanbury Brown–Twiss interferometer. We analyze timing jitter, photon antibunching, and the hallmark g⁽²⁾(0) < 0.5 signature.

Quantum Optics · Single-Photon Characterization
Dr. Swaroop Kumar Palai · Single-Photon Emission Benchmarking

How can an experimentalist unequivocally prove that an emitter produces genuine quantum light rather than faint classical attenuation? The definitive experimental test is the second-order intensity correlation function, g(2)(τ), measured using a Hanbury Brown–Twiss (HBT) interferometer.

In an HBT setup, photoluminescence from a targeted nano-object is directed through a 50:50 non-polarizing beam splitter onto two Single-Photon Avalanche Diodes (SPADs: Start and Stop channels). A Time-Correlated Single-Photon Counting (TCSPC) module records the histogram of arrival delay times τ = t2 − t1.

g(2)(τ) = 1 − [1 − g(2)(0)] e−|τ|/τ1,    g(2)(0) = 1 − (1/N)ρ2
Second-order correlation dip for N identical quantum emitters with background purity ρ = S/(S + B)

For classical electromagnetic fields, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality dictates that g(2)(0) ≥ 1 (coherent laser light yields g(2)(τ) = 1, whereas thermal light exhibits photon bunching g(2)(0) = 2). However, a true two-level quantum emitter cannot radiate two photons at the same instant; following emission, it must be re-excited before emitting again. Consequently, the simultaneous coincidence probability at zero delay drops to zero:

g⁽²⁾(0) < 0.5 &implies; Unambiguous Single-Photon Source (Antibunched)

When multiple emitters (N ≥ 2) reside within the confocal volume, g(2)(0) scales as 1 − 1/N, exceeding the quantum threshold. Real-world background luminescence (S/B) and detector timing jitter convolve the dip, making nanoscale engineering crucial for maximizing quantum purity.

Purcell Enhancement & Background Suppression

Coupling deterministically placed single nanodiamonds to optical metasurfaces accelerates the spontaneous emission rate via the Purcell effect, shortening excited state lifetimes down to <5 ns and elevating signal-to-noise ratios to yield record room-temperature antibunching dips (g(2)(0) < 0.12).
